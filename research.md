# research notes

These papers informed the reminder and the decision not to present the original camera experiment as a fatigue detector. This is a focused starting point, not a systematic review or clinical validation. Links go to PubMed abstracts; study settings and populations limit how broadly their results apply.

- [Talens-Estarelles et al. (2023), PMID 35963776](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35963776/): 29 symptomatic computer users used personalized 20-20-20 reminders for two weeks. Digital eye strain and dry eye symptoms decreased, but objective ocular surface measures did not change and symptom improvements did not persist after reminders stopped. This supports a reminder as a reasonable option, not a promise of prevention or treatment.
- [Redondo et al. (2025), PMID 40466853](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40466853/): 24 young adults completed four 40-minute reading conditions. More frequent or self-paced breaks were associated with less reported eye strain than no breaks. This supports allowing the interval to be adjusted and breaks to be taken early.
- [Biswas et al. (2026), PMID 42929271](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42929271/): a controlled gaming study in 100 healthy adults compared adherence and non-adherence to 20-20-20 over 40 minutes. Some vision parameters differed between groups; the short, specific setting does not establish long-term outcomes.
- [Portello et al. (2013), PMID 23538437](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23538437/): in 21 participants, incomplete blinks were associated with symptoms, while prompting a higher blink rate did not significantly change symptom scores. Blink count alone should not be treated as a diagnosis.
- [Real-Time Blink Detection as an Indicator of Computer Vision Syndrome (2023), PMID 36901579](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36901579/): an exploratory webcam study of 13 students found an association between blink rate and questionnaire scores. It does not validate the existing detector for individuals.
- [PERCLOS-based technologies for detecting drowsiness (2023), PMID 37193281](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37193281/): this review discusses eye closure as a drowsiness measure, with limitations in some populations and settings. Drowsiness and digital eye strain are different outcomes; PERCLOS is not a general eye-strain diagnosis.
- [Computer vision syndrome and its determinants (2022), PMID 36518554](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36518554/): a systematic review found associations between computer vision syndrome and factors including screen use and lack of breaks. Observational associations do not prove that this app prevents symptoms.
- [Computer vision syndrome: a review of ocular causes and potential treatments (2011), PMID 21480937](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21480937/): reviews multiple possible contributors, including dry eye and visual function, showing why a single camera signal is insufficient.

The app records self-reports as self-reports and reminders as reminders. It does not infer medical status from either. Future detection work should evaluate measurement accuracy, missing data, glasses, lighting, accessibility, consent and privacy, then validate any intended clinical use with appropriate experts.
